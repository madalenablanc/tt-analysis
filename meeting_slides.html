Experimental Setup — CMS + PPS

CMS Central Detector

  • Superconducting solenoid, B = 3.8 T, ⌀ 6 m
  • Silicon pixel + strip tracker (charged particles)
  • ECAL (lead-tungstate crystals) + HCAL (brass/scintillator)
  • Gas-ionization muon chambers
  • Tau reconstruction via DeepTau algorithm
  • Particle-flow (PF) event reconstruction

Precision Proton Spectrometer (PPS)

  • Tracking + timing detectors at ≈ 210 m from IP on both sides
  • Detects intact protons in Roman Pots (12σ from beam)
  • Measures ξ = (pi − pf)/pi (fractional momentum loss)
  • Constrains central system: MX² = s·ξ₁·ξ₂
  • Full pixel upgrade in 2018 → improved resolution
Key advantage: proton tag directly constrains the ττ system mass and rapidity, powerfully rejecting DY background

Analysis Strategy — μτh Channel

Signal topology

  • Two intact tagged protons (one per PPS arm)
  • Opposite-sign μ + τh pair, no extra hadronic activity
  • μ pT > 35 GeV, τh pT > 100 GeV, |η| < 2.4
  • Mass & rapidity matching between central system and protons

Backgrounds

SourceTreatment
Drell-Yan (DY)MC + PU proton enrichment
tt̄MC + PU proton enrichment
QCD multijetsData-driven (same-sign CR)

8-step workflow

Phase 0 — skimming: trigger, object selection, save proton branches
Phase 1 — event selection: IDs, kinematic cuts, sign selection
Proton info — real protons for data/QCD; PU pool enrichment for DY/tt̄
Normalization — L·σ/Σwgen, scale factors, proton acceptance (0.245)
Proton observables — ξ per arm, MX(proton), rapidity, matching variables
BDT training — 9 variables, central kinematics + CMS/PPS consistency
Shape histograms — mass or BDT score for signal / backgrounds / data
Combine — limits on r = σ/σSM, significance, best-fit r
